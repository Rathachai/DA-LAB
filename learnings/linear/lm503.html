How it works

  1. Window: every hour, the last w readings (x₁…xw) are used to predict the next reading (y). The window slides forward by 1 hour.
  2. Learn "healthy": a linear model y = β₀ + β₁x₁ + … + βwxw is fitted only on the first part of the record, when the machine is known to be healthy (chronological split).
  3. Monitor: afterwards the model keeps predicting one step ahead from the real readings. While the machine behaves as before, the residual (actual − predicted) stays small and noise-like.
  4. Alarm: when the machine starts to fail, reality drifts away from what "healthy" predicts and residuals grow. Raise an alarm if |residual| > k·σ, where σ is the residual spread seen during training (optionally m times in a row).
  5. Trade-off: small k = early warning but false alarms; large k = few false alarms but late or missed detection. Slow drifts are hard: the model partly follows a gentle trend, so its residual stays small. Try a window of 1 vs. 10 on each fault.

Try: gradual drift with a "constant" pattern vs. a "sine" pattern; growing variance with k = 2 vs. 5; then drag your own spike into the healthy part and watch a false alarm appear.
