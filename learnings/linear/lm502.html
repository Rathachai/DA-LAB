Each point = cost if θ were set to that health level. Low θ → repair planned too late → risk of breakdown. High θ → planned too early → wasted life. The blue curve is what you can compute before the machine fails; the dashed curve is the real cost in hindsight. When the trend is biased (e.g. accelerating wear) the two disagree — that is the risk of trusting a straight line.

How it works

  1. Sense: the health index is measured with noise every hour.
  2. Fit: a straight line health = β₀ + β₁·t is fitted to recent readings (gradient descent or the exact formula).
  3. Extrapolate: solve the line for the hour where health reaches θ → plan the repair. Solve for health = 0 → predicted failure → RUL.
  4. Decide: repair too early → you throw away useful life; too late → breakdown cost.

Try: Accelerating wear (a straight line is too optimistic), Fast early wear (too pessimistic), Step shocks, and compare a short vs. long fit window.
